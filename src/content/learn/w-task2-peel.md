Writing Task 2 carries **two-thirds of your total Writing score** and requires at least 250 words in 40 minutes. Plenty of candidates memorise impressive vocabulary yet stay stuck at Band 5.5–6.0 because their body paragraphs **list disconnected ideas**: every sentence throws in a new point without explaining or supporting the previous one.

:::rule The standard body paragraph structure (PEEL)
Each Task 2 body paragraph should focus on **1 (or at most 2) clear main ideas** developed through the four **PEEL** beats:
- **P (Point)**: A topic sentence stating the paragraph's main argument directly.
- **E (Explain)**: Explain *why* or *how* it works (cause, mechanism, or consequence).
- **E (Example)**: A concrete, realistic example (no invented statistics needed) to ground the point.
- **L (Link)**: Tie the argument clearly back to the essay question.
:::

## A 40-minute Task 2 workflow

:::steps
1. **Analyse the prompt & plan (5 minutes)**: Underline keywords, identify the exact question type (*Opinion*, *Discussion*, *Advantages/Disadvantages*, *Problem-Solution*, or *Two-part*), and decide your position up front.
2. **Introduction (3–4 minutes, ~40 words)**: Sentence 1 paraphrases the prompt's context; Sentence 2 (the thesis statement) answers the prompt directly.
3. **Body 1 & Body 2 (22 minutes, ~95 words each)**: Develop 1–2 ideas per paragraph with PEEL, using natural linkers (*Consequently*, *For instance*, *Conversely*).
4. **Conclusion (3–4 minutes, ~35 words)**: Restate your position and key points in fresh words—never introduce a brand-new argument here.
5. **Proofread (final 5 minutes)**: Check subject–verb agreement, articles, spelling, and do a quick word-count check to make sure you clear 250 words.
:::

## Listing ideas vs developing an idea (PEEL)

:::examples
✓ *Exposure to manageable risks helps young adults build resilience. When individuals step outside their comfort zone, they are forced to solve unexpected problems, which gradually strengthens their confidence in unfamiliar situations. For example, a graduate who moves abroad for a first job often adapts faster to workplace pressure than peers who remain at home.* // Flows logically from point → mechanism → concrete example.
✗ *Taking risks is very good because it makes people stronger, helps them earn more money, gives them new friends, and improves their career prospects in the future.* // Cramming four undeveloped ideas into one sentence caps Coherence and Task Response around Band 5.5.
:::

:::trap Going off-topic or ignoring half the prompt
In a **Discuss both views and give your own opinion** essay, exploring both sides while forgetting to state a clear **personal stance** (or tossing in a single vague line in the conclusion) severely penalises Task Response. Likewise, a **Two-part question** requires you to answer both questions fully.
:::

## Quick thesis & paragraph plans for 4 common essay types

| Essay type | Sample thesis (in Introduction) | How to split Body 1 & Body 2 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opinion (Agree/Disagree) | *While X has merits, I largely agree that...* | Body 1: Reason 1 · Body 2: Reason 2 (or Concession + Rebuttal) |
| Discussion + Opinion | *Although some favour A, I believe B is more practical.* | Body 1: View A · Body 2: View B + your own stance |
| Causes & Solutions | *This trend stems from several factors, which require joint action.* | Body 1: 2 Causes · Body 2: 2 Matching solutions |
| Advantages vs Disadvantages | *Despite notable drawbacks, the benefits of X are more significant.* | Body 1: Drawbacks · Body 2: Outweighing benefits |

> The prompt explicitly requires you to *"give your own opinion"*. Without a clear, consistent position throughout the essay, Task Response rarely rises above Band 5.5–6.0.
:::
