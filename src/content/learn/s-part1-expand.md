Speaking Part 1 lasts about 4–5 minutes and covers short, everyday questions about your life, hobbies, and routines. It is the easiest part to make a strong first impression, yet many candidates lose marks in **Fluency & Coherence** by giving clipped one-sentence replies or rambling like a memorised script.

:::rule The Part 1 sweet spot: 2 to 3 sentences (15–25 seconds)
- **Sentence 1**: Answer the question directly (using fresh phrasing instead of echoing the examiner's exact words).
- **Sentence 2**: Add one reason, feeling, or specific detail (*because / mainly since / what I love about it is...*).
- **Sentence 3 (optional)**: Add a quick example or a contrast in time/situation (*especially when... / unlike before...*).
:::

## The A-R-E formula (Answer – Reason – Example)

:::steps
1. **Listen closely to the question word and tense**: If the examiner asks *"Did you use to..."* (past) or *"Would you like to..."* (hypothetical), match that exact tense in your first sentence.
2. **Answer directly (Answer)**: Avoid saying *"Yes, I do"* and stopping. Paraphrase naturally: *"Definitely, I'm quite into..."* or *"Not really, to be honest..."*.
3. **Expand (Reason / Example)**: Attach a concrete personal reason so your answer sounds genuine and opens the door for natural vocabulary.
4. **Stop cleanly**: Once you have delivered 2–3 complete sentences, finish naturally so the examiner can move on to the next question.
:::

## Band 5.0 vs Band 7.0 answers compared

:::examples
✓ **Examiner**: *Do you enjoy outdoor activities?* → *"Yes, definitely. I try to go cycling around the lake near my home every Sunday because it really helps me clear my head after a stressful week of studying. It's much better than just staying indoors scrolling on my phone."* // Concise, natural, with idiomatic phrasing (*clear my head, scrolling on my phone*) and an effortless comparison.
✗ **Examiner**: *Do you enjoy outdoor activities?* → *"Yes, I enjoy outdoor activities. Outdoor activities are very good for health and help me relax."* // Repeats the examiner's exact phrase (*enjoy outdoor activities*) and sounds stiff and textbook-like.
:::

:::trap Don't memorise robotic filler openings
Long, rehearsed stalls like *"Well, that's a very interesting question that I haven't thought about before, let me think..."* immediately signal a memorised script to the examiner. If you need a second to think, keep it brief and natural: *"Hmm, let me see..."* or *"Actually, I'd say..."*.
:::

## Natural paraphrases for everyday topics

| What you mean | Flat phrasing | Higher-scoring natural phrasing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I like it a lot | *I like it very much.* | *I'm quite keen on... / It's right up my street.* |
| Sometimes | *Sometimes I do it.* | *Every now and then / Once in a while...* |
| Very busy | *I am very busy.* | *My schedule is pretty packed / I'm swamped with...* |
| Helps me relax | *It helps me relax.* | *It helps me unwind / take my mind off work.* |

:::check
The examiner asks: *"Did you enjoy learning history when you were at school?"* Which opening shows the most natural **Grammatical Range** and **Lexical Resource**?
- [ ] Yes, I enjoy history at school very much because history is interesting.
- [x] To be honest, not really. Back then, we mostly had to memorise dates and names, which felt a bit dry to me.

> The second option matches the past tense (*had to, felt*), sounds honest and conversational, and gives a concrete reason with natural phrasing (*felt a bit dry*).
:::
