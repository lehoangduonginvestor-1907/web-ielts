In IELTS Listening, hearing the right answer is only half the job. One misspelled letter, a missing plural *-s*, or mixing up 15 and 50 scores **0 points**. The most frustrating marks to lose are easy Part 1 items thrown away on spelling or number slips.

The good news is capitalisation is not penalised: lowercase or capital letters are both accepted. **Spelling**, however, must be 100% accurate.

## Letters and numbers that are easy to confuse

When a speaker spells out a street name, surname, or booking reference, a few sound pairs trip candidates up repeatedly:

| Confusable set | Key distinction | Quick listening cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A / E / I | A is /eɪ/, E is /iː/, I is /aɪ/ | Think of *day* (A), *see* (E), and *my* (I) |
| G / J | G is /dʒiː/, J is /dʒeɪ/ | G ends with an "ee" sound; J ends with an "ay" sound |
| M / N | M closes the lips at the end; N leaves them open | M sounds fuller and hummed; N is sharper |
| B / P / V | P has a strong puff of air; V vibrates teeth on lip | B and P use both lips; V has a soft buzzing sound |
| *double T* | Two identical letters in a row | Hear *double* → prepare to write the letter twice |
| 13 vs 30, 15 vs 50 | Stress falls on *-teen*, or on the first syllable of *-ty* | *fif-TEEN* stretches the ending; *FIF-ty* clips it short |

In phone numbers and reference codes, speakers usually say *"oh"* for 0 (rather than *zero*) and use *"double"* for two identical digits in a row (e.g. *"double seven"* for 77, *"double oh"* for 00).

## Prices, decimals, and dates

:::steps Formatting numbers and dates safely
1. For **prices**, include the currency symbol if it is not already printed before the blank (e.g. £4.50 or $12.80). Write *four pounds fifty* as 4.50, not 4.5.
2. For **decimals**, *"point"* marks the decimal dot (3.25 is spoken as *three point two five*).
3. For **dates**, formats like *14 March*, *14th March*, and *March 14* are all accepted. Still, watch the **word limit**: if it says "ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER", *14 March* is always safe, whereas *the 14th of March* exceeds the limit.
4. Always write digits (15) instead of spelling out numbers (*fifteen*) to avoid spelling errors and stay within word limits.
:::

## Plural endings and spelling accuracy

:::trap Missing a single -s still costs the mark
The speaker says: *"We have discounts for students."* If the prompt reads "Discounts are given to ____", the answer must be *students* with **-s**. Writing *student* breaks both the grammar of the note and the exact word in the recording.
:::

You don't need to worry about capital letters: *baker* and *Baker* both score. Dropping or swapping a single letter, however (such as *Febuary* missing *r*, or *accomodation* missing *m*), costs the entire point.

:::examples
✓ Audio: *"That's spelled M-A-C-D-O-N-A-L-D."* → Write *MacDonald* or *macdonald*. // Capitalisation is optional as long as every letter is right.
✓ Audio: *"Arrive on the twenty-third of August."* Limit: "ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER". → Write *23 August*. // Exactly one number and one word.
✗ Audio: *"Arrive on the twenty-third of August."* → Writing *the 23rd of August* when the limit is 2 words. // Extra *the* and *of* break the word limit.
:::

:::check
In a hotel booking audio, the receptionist reads a phone number: *"Oh-seven-four, double-nine, two-one-oh-six."*

Which number is correct?
- [ ] 07492106
- [x] 074992106
- [ ] 07499216
> *"Oh"* is 0, and *"double-nine"* means two 9s in a row (99). Notice there are two *"oh"* digits: one at the start and one near the end.
:::

:::check
The speaker gives an address: *"Live at forty-eight, Hillcrest Road. That's H-I-double L-C-R-E-S-T."* The word *Road* is already printed after the blank, and the limit is "ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER".

What should you write in the blank?
- [ ] 48 Hillcrest Road
- [x] 48 Hillcrest
- [ ] 48 Hilcrest
> *Road* is already printed on the sheet, so don't repeat it. *Hillcrest* has *double L* (two Ls), and paired with 48 it counts as one number and one word—right inside the limit.
:::

## Five-minute daily drill

If you regularly drop marks on Part 1 numbers and spelling, open the transcripts of past mistakes, pull out 10 everyday words you tend to misspell (like *Wednesday, February, library, environment, accommodation*), and drill them by hand each week. Practice jotting down rapid strings of spelled names and phone numbers too.
