Map and plan labelling usually appears in **Part 2**. You see a map or floor plan with lettered locations (A, B, C...) and a list of places to match to the right letters. The speaker will "walk you through" the map.

The vocabulary here is rarely difficult. The real challenge is listening and looking at the same time—get lost once and you can easily miss the next several questions.

## Before the audio starts

You get about 30 seconds. Spend all 30 seconds studying the map—don't re-read the instructions.

:::steps
1. Find the **starting point**: *You are here*, *Entrance*, *Main gate*, or an arrow. The speaker will almost certainly start there.
2. Check for a **compass**. If there is one, expect *north, south, east, west*. If not, expect *left, right, straight ahead*.
3. Read all **printed labels** on the map (*Car park, Lake, Reception*...). These are your landmarks—the speaker will locate the target places relative to them.
4. Spot where letters are **clustered together**. Two adjacent letters (e.g. D and E side by side) are where the test will set a trap.
5. Read the numbered list of questions. The speaker follows this list **in exact order**.
:::

## While listening

:::rule Put your pen on the map and walk along
Rest your pen tip (or cursor) on the starting point. Every time the speaker turns or moves forward, move along with them. You are walking across the map together, not sitting back and guessing.
:::

Speakers usually name the **target place first**, then give directions to it: *"Now, the café. If you go past the lake and turn left..."* The moment you hear the next place on your list, you know the previous question is over. If you missed it, **let it go** and lock onto the new one. Trying to replay the old question in your head is the fastest way to lose two more.

## Direction phrases to recognise instantly

| Category | Common phrases | Meaning / cue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moving | *go straight on, go past, carry on until, follow the path round* | Continue ahead, pass a landmark, or curve along a path |
| Turning | *turn left at, take the second right, bear right* | Turn at a junction, skip the first turn, or angle slightly right |
| Relative position | *next to, beside, opposite, behind, in front of, between A and B* | Position compared to a printed landmark |
| Corners & edges | *in the corner, at the far end, at the top/bottom, on the edge* | Extremes or outer borders of the map |
| Compass points | *in the north-east corner, to the south of, on the western side* | Use the compass rose rather than screen left/right |
| Before & after | *just before you reach, just after, the first building you come to* | Exact sequence along the route |

:::tip Left and right belong to the walker
*Turn left* means left **from the direction the speaker is facing**, not the left side of the page. If the speaker is walking from the top of the map toward the bottom, their "left" is on the **right** side of your screen. Mentally rotate the map to match their heading.
:::

## Two classic traps

:::trap Mentioning an old location first
"The gift shop **used to be** next to the entrance, but it's now moved **behind the café**." You hear *next to the entrance* first and feel tempted to pick it immediately. The answer is the final confirmed spot—wait for the end of the sentence, especially when you hear *used to, originally, was going to, but,* or *actually*.
:::

:::trap Two letters right next to each other
When D and E sit side by side on the same path, the speaker will add one small detail to separate them: "the **second** building on the right", "the one **nearer the lake**", "**just before** the bridge". That single detail decides the answer.
:::

:::check
You are standing at the south gate of a park, facing north. The speaker says: *"Walk straight ahead until you reach the fountain, then turn right. The café is the first building on your left."*

Where is the café relative to the fountain?
- [ ] West of the fountain, on the left side of the map
- [x] East of the fountain, on the north side of the path
- [ ] East of the fountain, on the south side of the path
> Facing north and turning right puts you heading **east**. While walking east, your left side is **north**. So the café is east of the fountain, on the north side of the path.
:::

:::check
The speaker says: *"We were going to put the playground beside the car park, but in the end it went in the far corner, behind the tennis courts."*

Where is the playground located?
- [ ] Beside the car park
- [x] In the far corner, behind the tennis courts
- [ ] Between the car park and the tennis courts
> *We were going to ... but in the end* shows the original plan was dropped. The final confirmed spot is in the far corner, behind the tennis courts.
:::

## If you get lost

It happens to everyone. The moment you lose track of where the speaker is:

1. Stop trying to piece together the current question.
2. Look at the question list and listen for the **next place name**.
3. When you hear it, find the first **printed landmark** they mention and place your pen back there.
4. At the end of the test, pick a best guess for any skipped question. A blank guarantees zero marks.
