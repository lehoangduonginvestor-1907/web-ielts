In Speaking Part 2, you are handed a Cue Card with 1 minute to prepare and up to 2 minutes to speak non-stop. Many candidates **run out of steam at the 45-second mark** because they try to write full sentences during prep time or stick too rigidly to the 3–4 short bullet points.

:::rule How to use your 1-minute prep wisely
- **Never write full sentences**: 60 seconds is only enough for 15–20 keywords, standout phrases, and your opening angle.
- **Turn bullet points into a story**: Cue card prompts usually follow *What / Where → Details / Experience → Feelings / Why*. Don't tick them off like a checklist.
- **Pick a memory rich in detail**: Talk about a real experience—or adapt a real memory slightly to make it easier to tell—as long as it stays natural and coherent.
:::

## A 4-stage arc to speak smoothly for 1:45–2:00

:::steps
1. **Stage 1: Quick setup (15–20 seconds)**: Name the person, place, or event right away, along with when and where it happened.
2. **Stage 2: Background & vivid details (35–40 seconds)**: Describe the setting, appearance, or what led up to it (lean on descriptive adjectives).
3. **Stage 3: A memorable moment or small twist (35–40 seconds)**: Zoom in on one concrete incident (e.g. a sudden downpour, getting lost, or your first impression). This extends your talk naturally without running dry.
4. **Stage 4: Feelings & lasting takeaway (20–30 seconds)**: Wrap up with how it made you feel, what you learned, or why it still sticks with you today.
:::

## How to jot notes in 60 seconds

:::examples
✓ *Kyoto, autumn 2024: crimson leaves, serene temple -> got lost -> friendly local -> unforgettable* // Just nouns, adjectives, and story arrows so a one-second glance keeps you flowing.
✗ *Today I am going to talk about a city that I visited. That city is Kyoto in Japan. I went there last year with my friends...* // Writing full sentences barely gets you past the intro, and you end up stumbling while reading off the page.
:::

:::trap Don't panic if the examiner stops you at 2 minutes
When 2 minutes are up, the examiner will say *"Thank you"* to stop you and ask one brief rounding-off question. That is **not** a sign you did poorly—it simply means you sustained your talk well for the full allotted time.
:::

## Natural signposts between stages in Part 2

| Stage | Natural signpost | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | *I'd like to talk about... which goes back to...* | Smooth launch that sets the timeframe |
| Adding detail | *What really stood out to me was...* | Highlights a vivid feature |
| Telling the moment | *Interestingly, right when we arrived...* | Expands the story with a concrete detail |
| Wrapping up | *Looking back, it was definitely a memorable...* | Adds personal reflection and closes neatly |
