This is the largest question group in IELTS Listening: form, note, table, sentence, and flow-chart completion. At Band 5, it is also the most reliable place to secure marks if you keep a steady pace.

The core rule: **the word you write in the gap must come directly from the recording**—do not change it to a synonym. By contrast, the printed words around the gap on the question paper are usually paraphrased from the audio.

## Follow the order and landmarks around each gap

In Listening completion tasks, answers always come **in the exact order of the questions**, from top to bottom—or left to right across a table. For tables, always read the **row and column headings** before listening so you know which category each gap belongs to.

:::steps Five steps for completion tasks
1. Check the **word limit** carefully (e.g. "ONE WORD ONLY" or "NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER").
2. If it is a table, read the **column headers and row labels** first, then look at individual gaps.
3. Underline **landmarks** right before or near each gap (names, dates, or the topic noun for that line).
4. Predict the **part of speech** and whether you need a singular or plural form.
5. While listening, wait for an equivalent meaning to the printed text, then catch the exact missing word and write it verbatim.
:::

:::examples
✓ Prompt: "Maximum weight allowed: ____ kg." Audio: *"You can't carry anything heavier than twenty-five kilos."* → Write *25*. // *Maximum weight allowed* becomes *can't carry anything heavier than*. Write the number you hear.
✗ Prompt: "Cost of membership: £____." Audio: *"It used to be forty pounds, wait, no, sorry, it's forty-five now."* → Writing *40*. // Writing the first number you hear without waiting for the speaker's self-correction.
:::

## Two traps that cost easy marks

:::trap Mid-sentence self-corrections
In Parts 1 and 2, speakers often state a detail and immediately correct themselves: *"Let's book the fifteenth... oh, no, sorry, let's make it the sixteenth."* Listen to the whole thought before locking in your answer—don't tune out the moment your pen hits the page.
:::

:::trap Extra words or the wrong word form
If the prompt reads "The museum opens in ____" with a limit of "ONE WORD ONLY" and the audio says *"It welcomes visitors in May"*, write only *May*. Writing *in May* repeats *in* and breaks the word limit. Likewise, if the audio says a plural (*tickets*) and the gap requires a plural, keep the *-s*.
:::

| Common mistake | Prompt & audio example | How to avoid it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Repeating a printed word | Prompt: "at ____". Audio: *at noon*. Writing *at noon* | Read the whole phrase with your answer to check for doubles like *at at* |
| Exceeding the word limit | Limit: "ONE WORD". Writing *old bridge* | Recount the words you wrote |
| Dropping a plural ending | Audio: *two workshops*. Writing *workshop* | Listen closely to final sounds and check the grammar of the note |

:::check
A table has three columns: *Course*, *Day*, and *Equipment needed*. In the *Pottery* row under *Equipment needed*, it says: "Bring an old ____."

The teacher says: *"For Tuesday's pottery class, we supply all the clay and tools, so just remember to bring an old shirt to keep your clothes clean."* Word limit: "ONE WORD ONLY". What is the correct answer?
- [ ] *tools*
- [x] *shirt*
- [ ] *old shirt*
> *an old* is already printed before the gap, so do not write *old* again. *Clay* and *tools* are supplied by the school; the item students need to *bring* is *shirt*.
:::

:::check
Note: "New library branch opens on ____." Word limit: "ONE WORD AND/OR A NUMBER".

The staff member says: *"We originally planned to open the new branch on 12 September, but the painting took longer than expected, so we pushed the opening back to 19 September."* What should you write?
- [ ] *12 September*
- [x] *19 September*
- [ ] *on 19 September*
> *originally planned* signals an old date that changed (*pushed back to*). Do not include *on* since it is already printed. *19 September* is one number and one word, which fits the limit.
:::

## Quick review after each question group

When transitioning to the next group or at the end of the recording, scan the gaps you just filled. Read your word together with the printed note to verify that the grammar flows naturally, check plural *-s* endings, and recount the words. Many small Part 1 slips can be fixed in this 10-second check.
