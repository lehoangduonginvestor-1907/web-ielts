Matching features asks you to link a list of statements or findings to a box of specific names—usually researchers, places, organisations, or historical periods.

Its biggest advantage is that **proper names are effortless to scan** because of capital letters or numbers. If you locate every single mention of each name upfront, you save huge amounts of time.

## Mark every single mention of every name

The most common mistake is reading the first sentence mentioning *Dr. Patel*, seeing no match for the current statement, and moving on—missing that *Patel* (or *she*) reappears two paragraphs later with the exact answer.

:::steps How to handle Matching features
1. Check the instruction line to see whether names may be **used more than once**. Remember that some names in the box may **not be used at all**.
2. Scan the entire passage and **underline or circle every occurrence** of every name in the box.
3. Watch for shortened forms such as a **surname only** (*Patel* for *Dr. Ananya Patel*), titles (*the lead researcher*), or pronouns (*he, she*) in the sentences right after the name.
4. Read the statements to match and underline their core ideas (they are guaranteed to be **paraphrased**).
5. Start with the name that appears only **once or twice** in the passage: read carefully around it and lock in its statements first.
:::

## Two classic misattribution traps

:::trap Missing a person's second appearance
A scientist may be quoted in paragraph B about experimental methods and quoted again in paragraph E commenting on the results. If you only marked paragraph B, you may assume a statement belongs to nobody there and assign it to the wrong name.
:::

:::trap A name is mentioned, not credited with the claim
Check carefully who owns each opinion. A sentence might read: *"While Smith argued that the soil was too dry, recent data collected by Lin showed adequate moisture."* Here, **Lin** proved the soil had enough moisture, while **Smith** claimed it was too dry. Skimming Smith's name at the start and jumping ahead assigns Lin's finding to Smith.
:::

:::examples
✓ Box: *A. Professor Grant*, *B. Dr. Sato*. Text: *"Professor Grant published the first survey of the cave in 1998. Five years later, he revised his estimate of its depth."* Statement: *updated earlier measurements*. → Pick *Grant*, because *he* in the next sentence refers back to Grant. // Track pronouns like *he/she* after a name.
✗ Later in the text: *"Dr. Sato later praised Grant's early maps."* Statement: *created the first maps of the cave*. → Picking *Sato* because *maps* sits next to Sato's name. // Grant made the maps; Sato only praised them.
:::

:::check
Paragraph: *"Historian Elena Vance suggests that the harbour wall collapsed due to poor mortar. However, engineer David Thorne, who examined the stones under a microscope, believes an underground spring weakened the foundations. Vance later agreed that water damage played a minor role, though she stood by her main point."*

Statement: *"identifies water beneath the ground as the primary cause of the damage."*
Who does this match?
- [ ] Elena Vance
- [x] David Thorne
- [ ] Neither of them
> *water beneath the ground* paraphrases *underground spring*. Vance only conceded that water played a *minor role* while sticking to *poor mortar*, whereas David Thorne argued the underground spring weakened the foundations.
:::

:::check
Same paragraph. Statement: *"acknowledges a secondary factor while maintaining the original view."*
Who does this match?
- [x] Elena Vance
- [ ] David Thorne
- [ ] Neither of them
> *acknowledges a secondary factor* matches *agreed that water damage played a minor role*, and *maintaining the original view* matches *stood by her main point*. Notice she is referenced in the third sentence only by surname (*Vance*) and pronoun (*she*).
:::

## When one name appears everywhere

If one name pops up across four or five paragraphs, **save that name for last**. Clear out all the names that appear only once or twice first. Once you have crossed off most of the statements, only a handful remain—and you will know exactly what to look for inside that final person's sections.
