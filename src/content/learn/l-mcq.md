In Listening multiple choice, you have to read longer question stems while comparing three options (A, B, C) in real time. If you pick whichever option contains a word you just heard, you will walk straight into distractors.

The key principle: **speakers usually mention all three options**, but only one actually answers the question and survives as the final decision.

## Listen for the final decision, not the first mention

In conversations, speakers frequently suggest an idea, hesitate, and then revise or reject it. Transition words that signal a change of mind are your most important cues.

| Signal type | Common phrases in the audio | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rejecting or correcting | *but, however, actually, in fact* | The point just mentioned is overturned or modified |
| Abandoned plan | *I was going to, originally planned, used to* | An old option—do not pick it |
| Final decision | *in the end, on second thoughts, let's go with* | The idea right after this is usually the real answer |
| Reason for ruling out | *too expensive, closed on Mondays, sold out* | The option just mentioned cannot be used |

:::steps How to handle Listening multiple choice
1. During the preview time, read the **question stem** carefully to see who it asks about and which timeframe (past, present, or future plan).
2. Underline one or two short keywords in each option (A, B, C) so you can tell them apart at a glance.
3. While listening, don't grab the first option you hear. Wait to see if the speaker **rejects** or **revises** it with *but*, *actually*, or *in the end*.
4. Watch for **paraphrasing**: the correct option usually uses different words from the audio, while distractors often repeat an exact phrase you heard.
:::

:::examples
✓ Prompt: *Why did Maya choose the riverside flat?* Audio: *"The city-centre one was closer to work, and the garden flat was really cheap, but I picked the riverside place because it gets plenty of natural light."* → Pick the option about light (*well-lit* or *sunny*). // The other two flats are mentioned only to be ruled out.
✗ Prompt: *What will the club buy first?* Option A: *new tents*. Audio: *"We'd love to buy new tents, though we really can't afford them yet, so let's start with two small stoves."* → Picking *new tents* just because you heard the exact phrase. // *can't afford them yet* eliminates *new tents*.
:::

## "Choose TWO" questions

:::trap The two answers can appear in any order
When choosing two letters out of five (A to E), the speaker won't go alphabetically from A to B. They might mention E first, rule out B, and then confirm C as the second answer. Never assume the second answer sits below the first letter.
:::

For these questions, skim all five options before the audio starts and underline the core idea of each. While listening, put a tick next to agreed options and a cross next to rejected ones.

:::check
Two friends are discussing cooking classes.
Man: *"I wanted to sign up for the baking course on Saturdays, you know, to make bread."*
Woman: *"Same here, I was going to book that one, until I saw it was full. On second thoughts, the pasta workshop sounds fun, and it still has spaces."*

Which class do they decide to book?
- [ ] The baking course
- [x] The pasta workshop
- [ ] Both classes
> *I was going to* signals an abandoned plan because it was *full*. *On second thoughts* introduces their final choice: the *pasta workshop*.
:::

:::check
A museum director says: *"Our volunteer numbers are slightly lower than last year, so we expected ticket income to fall. Actually, sales have risen because of the weekend family discount, although repair costs for the roof remain our biggest headache."*

What caused ticket revenue to increase?
- [ ] A rise in volunteer numbers
- [x] The weekend family discount
- [ ] Lower roof repair costs
> All three topics (volunteers, tickets, roof) appear in the speech. Volunteers are *lower*, roof repairs are the *biggest headache*, and the real cause of rising sales (*sales have risen*) is the *weekend family discount*.
:::

## When you miss a question

Multiple choice is text-heavy, so it is easy to lose momentum. If the audio has moved on to keywords from the next question and you still haven't chosen, **pick your best guess immediately** (or mark it to revisit) and shift your eyes down right away. Staying locked in for the next three questions matters far more than dwelling on one that has already passed.
